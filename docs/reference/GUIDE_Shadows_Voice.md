The Shadows Voice & Style Guide
How to write, teach, and explain Shadows so it always sounds like the world speaking — not the designers.

## Purpose of This Guide
This document exists to keep the voice of Shadows consistent across all parts of the Core Rulebook, GM Workshop, and future supplements.
Shadows is not written like a neutral instruction manual. It is written like a world that knows what it is, knows what it does to people, and is honest about the consequences.
This guide is meant to be used: - while drafting new sections - while reviewing each other’s work - while resolving disagreements about tone or phrasing
If you ever ask, “Does this sound like Shadows?” — this is where you check.

## 1. Who Is Speaking?

### Core Rule
Shadows always speaks as the world itself — never as a detached designer or instructor.
The speaker is not: - Ken explaining design intent - Scott teaching a workshop - A neutral rules narrator
The speaker is: > NYTE City. The system. The rules as natural law.

### Strong Examples (Already in the Text)
“NYTE City is not a backdrop. It is an active participant.”
“We don’t stop you from making bad choices. We just make sure you live with them.”
“Preparation doesn’t make you safe. It makes you less surprised.” (GM Workshop tone)

### Weaker (Out-of-Voice) Pattern
“We recommend that the GM…”
This sounds like a designer giving advice about Shadows, not Shadows telling you how it works.

### Rewrite Pattern
Instead of: > “We recommend telegraphing danger clearly to players.”
Use: > “In NYTE City, danger that isn’t visible feels unfair. Danger that’s visible feels earned.”

## 2. Sentence Posture: Assert, Don’t Suggest

### Core Rule
Shadows asserts truths. It does not hedge, soften, or over-qualify.
The world is crowded. It is dangerous. It reacts.
The text should sound confident — even when explaining flexibility.

### Strong Examples
“Violence solves problems quickly but creates bigger ones.”
“Reputation travels faster than bullets.”
“No result replaces the fiction — it pushes it forward.”

### Weaker Pattern
“Violence can sometimes lead to additional complications.”
This is accurate — but timid.

### Rewrite Pattern
Replace: - can - might - sometimes
With: - does - will - tends to
Shadows speaks with earned confidence.

## 3. Teaching Through Truths, Not Advice

### Core Rule
Shadows teaches by describing how the world behaves, not by offering tips.
Players and GMs are trusted to adapt.

### Strong Examples
“The city doesn’t judge your choices, but it does remember them.”
“Difficulty is communicated, not calculated.”
“An enemy is an NPC who decided you crossed a line.”
These lines teach play philosophy without ever saying “you should.”

### Weaker Pattern
“GMs should try to avoid railroading players.”
This is true — but external.

### Rewrite Pattern
Instead of: > “GMs should avoid railroading.”
Use: > “Plans that depend on player obedience don’t survive contact with NYTE City.”

## 4. Reassurance Without Softness

### Core Rule
Shadows supports the reader without coddling them.
Fear and uncertainty are acknowledged — but not apologized for.

### Strong Examples
“This is not a heroic world. It is a reactive one.”
“You will not use every tool in this section right away. No one does.”
“The rest will be there when NYTE City gives you a reason to reach for it.”

### Weaker Pattern
“Don’t worry if this feels overwhelming.”
This sounds like a workshop facilitator, not the city.

### Rewrite Pattern
Instead of: > “Don’t worry if this feels like a lot.”
Use: > “This will feel like a lot at first. The city doesn’t explain itself either.”

## 5. Lists and Mechanics Are Laws of Nature

### Core Rule
Mechanics are not arbitrary systems — they are how pressure manifests.
Rules should feel inevitable.

### Strong Examples
“Luck is finite. When it runs out, the dice stop caring how important the moment is.”
“Health is not an on/off switch. It is a slow accumulation of pain, injury, and bad decisions catching up to you.”

### Weaker Pattern
“Luck is a meta-currency players can spend to influence dice.”
Correct — but sterile.

### Rewrite Pattern
Introduce mechanics with fiction-first framing, then explain procedure.

## 6. Humor: Dry, Rare, In-World

### Core Rule
Humor in Shadows is a pressure valve — not a punchline.
It should: - sharpen tension - reveal cynicism - never undercut stakes

### Strong Examples
“Also, Kpop. The Fae own that.”
“Being broke isn’t failure. It’s a complication — and NYTE City loves complications.”

### Guidance
If the humor sounds like it’s winking at the reader outside the world, it’s probably wrong.

## 7. Repetition Is Intentional

### Core Rule
Shadows repeats its core ideas on purpose.
Repetition reinforces worldview: - The city pushes back - Choices create consequences - Pressure reveals character

### Strong Examples
These ideas appear in: - the Introduction - the GM Workshop - the core mechanics
That’s a feature, not redundancy.

## 8. Pedagogy: Teaching Someone to Think in Shadows

### Core Rule
Shadows teaches judgment, not just procedure.
Every teaching moment should follow this order:
State a truth about the world
Show how it appears in play
Let the reader infer best practice

### Strong Example (GM Workshop Pattern)
Truth: > “Preparation is readiness, not control.”
Manifestation: > Example session prep focused on situations, NPC goals, and pressure.
Inference: > The GM learns when to stop prepping — without being told explicitly.

## 9. Voice Alignment Checklist (Use This in Review)
Before approving a paragraph, ask:
Does this sound like advice, or like a law of the world?
Is the authority coming from explanation, or consequence?
Could this sentence exist inside NYTE City?
Are we trusting the reader, or over-directing them?
If it sounds like NYTE City talking — it’s right.

## Final Note
Shadows is at its best when it sounds honest, confident, and unflinching.
Not cruel. Not smug. Not instructional.
Just a world that pushes back — and tells you so up front.
